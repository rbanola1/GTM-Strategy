# Sequence design

## Structure (three steps)
| Step | Day | Purpose | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | The signal and a single question | Under 80 words |
| 2 | 3 | A useful asset: protocol, application note or data point | Under 60 words |
| 3 | 6 | A light close: offer a sample, a call, or permission to stop | Under 40 words |

## Rules for the copy
- **First line = the signal**: a recent paper, grant, hire, event or website visit that explains why we are writing.
- One ask per email.
- Plain text, no images, no heavy formatting, a single link at most.
- Personalise from evidence, not from guesses. If there is no signal, the contact belongs in nurture, not in a sequence.
- Offer something useful (data, protocol, sample) before asking for a meeting.
- Include a simple way to opt out.

## Targeting
- Tier A and B contacts only; tier C goes to nurture.
- One decision-maker plus at most one escalation contact per account.
- Exclude anyone who replied in the last 90 days or is in another sequence.

## Review before sending
A person reads every first-step email for the first batch of a new sequence; after approval, the same template can run at a steady pace.

## A/B testing
Change one variable at a time (subject, first line, offer), send each variant to equal small groups, decide after at least about 100 sends per variant, and record the winner.
