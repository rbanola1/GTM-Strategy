# Sequence basics: practical rules

## Building a sequence
1. Create the email templates first.
2. Create the sequence: a **dynamic** sequence adjusts follow-up by engagement (opens, clicks); a **static** sequence sends the same steps to everyone.
3. Enrol contacts and schedule the emails.

## Rules from the data
- **Two emails per sequence.** Three touches irritated readers in practice.
- **Timing:** start on Monday or Tuesday and run to Wednesday or Thursday. Avoid Friday sends unless the list is very large and fresh.
- **Who to enrol:** use a dynamic sequence and prefer contacts who opened and clicked on earlier emails.
- **Auto-unenrol** anyone who gives a meaningful reply to the first email, and set a task to follow up later; monitoring replies by hand is not workable at high volume.
- **Who uses it:** inside sales for warm-lead generation; field sales after expos, to save time on a generic follow-up.

## Why this matters
These rules are what turned a high-volume, low-reply pattern into a paced, targeted one: send caps, a stop rule and a real-reply measure sit on top of them (see the main project).
